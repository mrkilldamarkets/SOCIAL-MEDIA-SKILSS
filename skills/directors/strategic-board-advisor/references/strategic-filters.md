# Strategic Filters — Board Questions, Focus & Acquisition

## The Board Question Set

Ask these regularly — not just during crises. The best time to identify problems is before they become urgent.

### What Are We Avoiding?
The things that don't get discussed in meetings are often the most important.
What uncomfortable truth is the organization not confronting?
What decision keeps getting deferred and why?

### What Are We Overestimating?
Revenue projections. Execution speed. Market size. Customer willingness to pay.
Where is optimism masquerading as analysis?
What assumption, if wrong, would fundamentally change the plan?

### What Are We Underestimating?
Execution complexity. Time required. Competitive response. Customer acquisition cost.
Where is the plan thinner than it looks?
What has surprised similar operators in similar situations?

### What Is the Highest-Leverage Move?
Of everything that could be done — what single action creates the most downstream value?
Is that the thing currently receiving the most attention and resources?
If not — why not?

### What Should We Stop Doing?
Most organizations accumulate activities. Few eliminate them.
What is consuming resources without producing commensurate value?
What would happen if we stopped doing it entirely?

### What Should We Double Down On?
What is working better than expected that isn't receiving proportional investment?
Where is the return on attention already proven — and being underexploited?

## Focus Protection Framework

The most dangerous strategic threat to 4x Heist is not competition — it's diffusion.

**Signs of dangerous diffusion:**
- More than three Priority 1 initiatives active simultaneously
- New ideas consistently displacing existing commitments
- Team (or founder) unable to articulate the single most important priority this week
- Revenue is growing but profit isn't (complexity is eating margin)
- "We need to do X" conversations that never reach a decision

**Focus protection rules:**
1. No new major initiative launches while an existing Priority 1 is incomplete or stalled
2. The question "should we also do X?" is answered with: "What does X replace?"
3. Monthly: eliminate or formally deprioritize one thing that isn't moving
4. Quarterly: run a full audit of all active projects — kill anything without a defined owner and timeline

**The subtraction question:**
"What would have to be true for us to confidently stop doing this?"
If the answer is "never" — examine the assumption that this is essential.

## Strategic Filter (Pre-Commitment)

Before committing time, capital, or attention to anything:

```
1. Does this generate revenue?                    Y / N
2. Does this build assets?                        Y / N
3. Does this strengthen the ecosystem?            Y / N
4. Does this create leverage?                     Y / N
5. Does this improve positioning?                 Y / N
6. Would this still matter in three years?        Y / N

Minimum threshold: 4 of 6 must be YES to proceed.
If fewer than 4: deprioritize without guilt.
If exactly 4: proceed only if it doesn't displace a stronger initiative.
If 5 or 6: priority candidate — evaluate against current stack.
```

## Acquisition & Launch Filter

Before launching a new brand, product, or acquiring any asset:

```
1. Can this become a meaningful asset (not just a revenue line)?
2. Can it scale without proportional founder time?
3. Can it operate without founder dependence at maturity?
4. Does it generate cash flow — or does it consume it?
5. Does it fit the ecosystem and strengthen it?
6. Would a holding company buy this as a standalone business?
```

**All six must be yes for a new brand or major acquisition.**
Three or more nos: this is a product, not an asset. Treat it accordingly.

**Hard boundary:** No new brand launches until the existing brands are each profitable
and have at least one non-founder operator managing day-to-day execution.

## Brand Protection Filter

Before any action that touches the JorDache Darling or 4x Heist brand publicly:

```
The Five-Year Test:
  In five years, will this look like the right decision?
  If yes: proceed.
  If uncertain: pause and investigate.
  If no: do not proceed regardless of short-term upside.

The Investor Test:
  If a sophisticated investor or capital partner saw this tomorrow — would it strengthen or weaken trust?

The Legacy Test:
  Does this move toward the operator/institutional figure positioning — or back toward influencer?

The Regret Test:
  In the worst outcome scenario for this decision — is that outcome acceptable and recoverable?
```

**Non-negotiables for brand protection:**
- Never publicly attack competitors or individuals by name
- Never chase trends that conflict with Sovereign Operator positioning
- Never discount premium offers to fill short-term revenue gaps
- Never associate the brand with partners who fail the five-year credibility test
- Never prioritize reach over trust in any distribution decision

## Capital Allocation Review

For any capital deployment above $5,000, run this review:

```
CAPITAL ALLOCATION REVIEW

Amount:               $[X]
Purpose:              [Specific use]
Category:             Business Operations / Revenue Expansion / Asset Acquisition / Reserves / Investment / Lifestyle

Evaluation:
  Expected return:    $[X] over [X months] — or [non-financial return description]
  Time to return:     [X months]
  Probability:        [High / Medium / Low — honest]
  Alternative use:    What else could this capital do?
  Asymmetry:          Upside: $[X] / Downside: $[X] — is the asymmetry favorable?
  Reversibility:      Can this decision be undone? At what cost?

Decision:             Approve / Reject / Modify
Rationale:            [One to two sentences — honest]
```

**Capital protection rules:**
- Never deploy reserves for anything other than genuine operational emergencies
- No lifestyle spend from capital that hasn't been earned as profit (not revenue)
- Any single deployment above $10,000 requires running this review formally before committing
