# Video Production — Architecture, Retention & Scripting

## Video Architecture

Every video contains six components in this order:

```
1. HOOK         — First 30 seconds. Create curiosity, establish value, give a reason to stay.
2. STORY        — Ground the viewer in what's happening. Context without padding.
3. EDUCATION    — The substance. What the viewer learns or gains.
4. INSIGHT      — The angle only JorDache can give. Operator lens. Not generic information.
5. APPLICATION  — How the viewer applies this. Make it actionable.
6. CTA          — One clear next step. Not multiple.
```

Every video answers four questions:
- What is happening?
- Why does it matter?
- What can the viewer learn?
- How does this support the operator journey?

## Retention Philosophy

Retention is king. Not views. Not subscribers. Retention.

YouTube rewards watch time. Watch time is earned by people staying. People stay for:

**Curiosity** — "I need to know how this ends"
**Story** — "I'm invested in this person's journey"
**Transformation** — "I'm watching someone change"
**Progress** — "I can see this moving forward"
**Conflict** — "There's a real problem being solved"
**Resolution** — "I need to see how this resolves"

Every video should have at least three of these active simultaneously.

**Retention checkpoints:**
- 30 seconds: viewer decides to stay or leave — hook must be complete
- 30%: first major retention drop — story must be fully established
- 50%: second retention drop — education peak must hit here
- 70%: viewers who reach 70% usually finish — close strong
- End: CTA window — only viewers who watched deserve a CTA

## First 30 Seconds Framework

The first 30 seconds determine whether the video lives or dies.

**Must establish all four:**

```
CURIOSITY:   Give them a reason to want to know what happens next.
             "By the end of this video, I'll show you exactly how I..."
             "Most traders never figure this out. I'm going to show you why."

VALUE:       Tell them what they're getting. Specifics over promises.
             "You'll walk away knowing exactly how to [specific outcome]"
             Not: "In this video we're going to talk about..."

NARRATIVE:   Drop them into the story. Don't build up to it.
             Start mid-action or mid-thought. Create immediate investment.

REASON TO STAY: The open loop. Create a question the video will answer.
             "...and the answer surprised me." / "...here's what I found."
```

**What never opens a video:**
- Channel logo animation
- "What's up everyone, welcome back to the channel"
- Long disclaimer sequences
- Slow explanation of what the video is about
- Recapping previous videos in the first 30 seconds

## Scripting Structure (Full Video)

### For Documentary / Vlog Style Videos (Operator Diaries series)

```
COLD OPEN (0:00–0:30)
  Start in the middle of something happening.
  "It's 6am in Da Nang and I just got off a trade that changed how I think about Gold."
  No intro. No greeting. Drop straight in.

CONTEXT ESTABLISH (0:30–2:00)
  Now ground the viewer. Who, what, where.
  Short. Not a life story — just enough to understand the stakes.

THE PROBLEM / SITUATION (2:00–5:00)
  What is the challenge, decision, or moment being documented?
  Be honest. If it was hard, say it was hard.

THE PROCESS (5:00–12:00)
  What happened. What was done. How it was navigated.
  This is where education lives — but it should feel like story, not a tutorial.

THE INSIGHT / LESSON (12:00–15:00)
  What did this reveal? What changed?
  The operator lens. Not generic wisdom — specific to the journey.

THE APPLICATION (15:00–17:00)
  How can the viewer use this?
  One to three specific takeaways — not a list of ten.

CTA (17:00–17:30)
  One action. Not three.
  "If this was useful, [subscribe / join the community / DM me one word]"
  No begging. No "smash that like button."
```

### For Educational / Trading Videos (Trading Intelligence series)

```
HOOK (0:00–0:30)
  Contrarian statement or surprising insight. Open with the conclusion.
  "Most traders are reading market structure backwards. Here's what they're missing."

PROBLEM FRAMING (0:30–2:00)
  What's the common mistake or misunderstanding?
  Show the viewer they might be experiencing this.

FRAMEWORK INTRODUCTION (2:00–4:00)
  Name the concept. Give it structure.
  Visual if possible (screen share, whiteboard, chart overlay).

BREAKDOWN (4:00–12:00)
  Step by step. Examples from real price action.
  Not hypothetical — actual XAUUSD moves with context.

COMMON MISTAKES (12:00–14:00)
  What goes wrong when people try to apply this?
  This section is gold for retention — people want to avoid being the mistake.

APPLICATION (14:00–16:00)
  How to use this starting now. Concrete. Specific.

CTA (16:00–16:30)
  One action. Direct. No multiple asks.
```

## B-Roll and Visual Direction

**Approved visual settings:**
- Clean workspace / home office (Da Nang, Bali environments)
- Trading screens (in-use, not staged)
- City environments (streets, architecture — cinematic framing)
- Training / gym footage (for operator lifestyle content)
- Travel and movement footage (airports, streets, new environments)
- Nature / water footage (aligned with Blue Marlin and Bahamas identity)

**Visual aesthetic:** Cinematic. High contrast. Warm or cool color grade — not flat.
Never looks like a YouTube bedroom studio.

**On-camera standard:** Fitted clothing, clean backgrounds, intentional framing.
Lighting must be intentional — ring light in a dark bedroom is not the standard.

**Screen recording standards:**
- Clean desktop (no clutter, no personal notifications visible)
- Chart platform with clean, minimal indicators
- No MT4/MT5 retail aesthetics for Blue Marlin content
