# Video Architecture — Structure, Hooks & Retention

## Retention Philosophy

Retention is king. Everything else is secondary.

People stay for: Curiosity / Story / Transformation / Progress / Conflict / Resolution

A video with 60% retention and 10K views builds more authority than a video with 20% retention
and 100K views. The algorithm rewards retention. Authority comes from completion.

**Target benchmarks:**
- First 30 seconds: retain 80%+ of viewers who clicked
- 50% retention mark: mid-video hook required
- Average view duration: 50%+ of total video length (aim for 55–60%)

---

## Video Architecture (Every Video)

```
HOOK           (0:00–0:30)   Stop the scroll. Establish the narrative. Create urgency to stay.
STORY SETUP    (0:30–2:00)   Context. Stakes. Why this video exists right now.
EDUCATION      (2:00–X:XX)   The core value — knowledge, framework, insight, journey.
INSIGHT        (Throughout)  Unexpected perspective, operator-level thinking, contrarian truth.
APPLICATION    (Near end)    What the viewer does with this — specific, not vague.
CTA            (Final 30s)   One action. Not five. Simple and direct.
```

**Pacing rule:** Every 90–120 seconds of a video needs a new visual, a new idea, or a narrative
development. Flat sections kill retention. If nothing changes in the edit for 2 minutes — cut it.

---

## The First 30 Seconds — Non-Negotiable Framework

The first 30 seconds determine whether someone stays or leaves.

**What must happen:**
1. Establish immediate curiosity or tension ("Here's what happened..." / "Most traders get this wrong...")
2. Signal what the viewer will get if they stay (implicit — not "in this video I'll show you")
3. Begin the story or insight — never defer it

**Never start with:**
- Long intros or channel logos
- "Hey guys, welcome back to the channel"
- Disclaimers (legal or otherwise)
- A slow explanation of what the video is about
- B-roll without audio that signals the content

**Instead start with:**
- The moment of conflict or insight, already in progress
- A statement that creates immediate tension or curiosity
- A visual or situation that demands an explanation

**Examples of strong opens:**
- "I just got off a call with an investor who almost pulled $50,000. Here's what happened."
- "Three months ago I made a decision that changed how I think about capital. I was wrong about almost everything."
- "Most traders will never pass a funded account. Not because they can't trade — because of this."

## Retention Mechanics — Mid-Video

Retention drops most sharply at three points:
- 15–30 seconds (first filter: does this deserve my time?)
- At the natural "end feeling" (when the viewer thinks the main point was made)
- Final third (when dopamine has been delivered and curiosity fades)

**Retention tools:**

**Pattern interrupts:** Unexpected cuts, tone shifts, visual changes — deployed every 2 minutes

**Open loops:** Introduce a question or tension that won't be resolved until later in the video
"I'll show you exactly how this played out — but first you need to understand why most people miss this entirely."

**Progress markers:** Let the viewer know where they are in the journey
"By the time we get to the last trade, everything I just explained will make sense."

**Re-hooks:** Mini-hooks at the 50% mark to retain viewers through the back half
"What I'm about to show you is the part most people skip — and it's the most important."

**Callback:** Reference something from the beginning at the end — creates a satisfying arc

## CTA Framework

One CTA per video. Maximum.

**Priority order for CTAs:**
1. Community (Telegram, Discord) — builds the ecosystem directly
2. Subscription with specific reason ("subscribe if you want to follow this journey")
3. Another video (keep them in the ecosystem)
4. Product/offer (only when the video's content naturally leads there)

**CTA delivery:**
- Natural, not desperate
- Specific reason ("join the Telegram — I post the analysis behind these videos there")
- Brief — 15–20 seconds maximum
- Never beg for likes or "smash the bell"

**What not to do:**
- "Don't forget to like, comment, and subscribe" (generic, low conversion, low credibility)
- Multiple CTAs competing for attention
- CTA before the value has been delivered

## Video Formats by Length

### Short-Form (8–12 minutes) — Insight / Analysis / Opinion
Best for: Market analysis, trading insights, contrarian takes, quick lessons
Structure: Hook → Point → Evidence → Application → CTA
Cadence: 2–3 per month

### Mid-Form (15–25 minutes) — Education / Breakdown / Tutorial
Best for: Full trade breakdowns, strategy explanations, operator system explanations
Structure: Full video architecture above
Cadence: 1–2 per month

### Long-Form (30–60 minutes) — Documentary / Journey / Deep Dive
Best for: Operator Diaries, building 4x Heist series, transformation stories
Structure: Full narrative arc — beginning, middle, end. Chapter markers.
Cadence: 1 per month minimum

### Vlog-Style (10–20 minutes) — Day in the Life / Behind the Build
Best for: Da Nang/Bali content, travel as lifestyle infrastructure, authentic operator life
Structure: Narrative-led. Events of the day with operator framing throughout.
Cadence: As location and lifestyle allow
