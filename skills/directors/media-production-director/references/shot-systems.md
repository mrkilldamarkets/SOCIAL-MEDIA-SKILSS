# Shot Systems — Philosophy, B-Roll & Production Protocol

## Shot Philosophy

Every shot must answer four questions before the camera rolls:

1. Why does this matter to the story?
2. What does this communicate about the Sovereign Operator?
3. What emotion does this create in the viewer?
4. What narrative does this support?

If a shot cannot answer all four — it is a filler shot. Filler shots dilute the library.

---

## Coverage System

For every filming session, capture all five layers:

```
WIDE SHOT      — Establishes context. Where are we. What is happening.
MEDIUM SHOT    — The operator in the environment. Connection and scale.
CLOSE SHOT     — Hands, face, screen, details. Intimacy and specificity.
DETAIL SHOT    — Textures, objects, environment elements. Premium feel.
TRANSITION     — Movement in and out of scenes. Doors, corridors, stairs, streets.
```

**Rule:** Never rely on a single shot type. A sequence requires at least three layers.
A scene built on only wide shots has no depth. A scene built on only close shots has no context.

## Priority Shot Categories

### Trading & Markets

```
Priority shots:
- Hands on keyboard during live market hours
- Chart screen with clean minimal indicators (no clutter)
- Multiple screen setup (wide, then close on specific chart)
- Journal writing (physical or digital)
- Pre-session preparation ritual
- Post-trade reflection moment
- Research reading (books, reports, screens)
- Market notes or planning sheets

What to avoid:
- Charts showing specific P&L numbers (unless intentional and contextualized)
- MT4/MT5 retail aesthetics for Blue Marlin-positioned content
- Celebrating wins ostentatiously
```

### Business & Building

```
Priority shots:
- Strategy sessions (whiteboard, notebook, laptop)
- Call setups (headphones, clean environment, professional framing)
- Planning documents and systems (organized, premium feel)
- Building moments (coding, designing, writing)
- Thinking sequences (window view, coffee, intentional stillness)
- Collaboration (where applicable, anonymized if needed)

What to avoid:
- Staged "working" shots that look posed
- Showing specific client information or financials without consent
```

### Fitness & Training

```
Priority shots:
- Warm-up sequences (movement, intentional)
- Compound lifts (squat, deadlift, press — effort visible but controlled)
- Accessory work (cables, machines — shows the system, not just the ego lift)
- Between-set recovery (breath, focus, mental reset)
- Post-session state (authentic — not posed gym mirror selfie)
- Morning routine preparation (pre-gym ritual)
- Nutrition preparation (meal prep, macro tracking)

What to avoid:
- Ego lifting (excessive weight poorly executed)
- Gym selfies or mirror content
- Attention-seeking behavior — discipline is silent
- Shirtless content unless directly relevant to competition prep (Legacy By Darling context)
```

### Travel & Movement

**Airport:**
```
- Arrival and departure sequences (purposeful movement)
- Window shots (aircraft, runway, sky)
- Waiting area work sessions (laptop, journal, preparation)
- Walking sequences through terminal (operator in motion)
- Gate moments (reflection, planning, quiet focus)
- Luggage transitions (movement between environments)
```

**Hotel:**
```
- Desk setup (clean, intentional — laptop, water, minimal)
- Work sessions at window (city view behind, operator in foreground)
- Morning routine (alarm, movement, first light)
- City views (wide establishing shots through window or balcony)
- Night sessions (lamp lit, focused — premium atmosphere)
- Environment details (hotel quality, city context, geographic identity)
```

**City:**
```
- Streets and architecture (cinematic wide angles)
- Walking sequences (operator in environment — not tourist in environment)
- Local infrastructure (markets, ports, skylines — geographic authenticity)
- Transition shots (entering/exiting spaces, vehicles, elevators)
```

### Lifestyle

```
Priority shots:
- Reading sequences (book, physical — not screen)
- Coffee/tea rituals (morning routine texture)
- Clean, minimal living environments
- Evening review or journaling
- Nature or water (ocean, rivers, landscapes — aligned with Sovereign identity)
- Intentional stillness moments (operator at rest — not idle)

What to avoid:
- Excessive luxury display for its own sake
- Restaurant content that looks like influencer dining
- Any content that makes freedom look lazy rather than earned
```

## Pre-Production Protocol

Before any filming session:

```
PRODUCTION BRIEF:

Session Name:       [e.g., "Da Nang Morning Routine — Week 3"]
Story Objective:    [What narrative does this session support?]
Shot List:          [Minimum 10 specific shots listed before arriving on location]
Location:           [Where exactly — what permissions needed]
Time of Day:        [Lighting consideration — golden hour, midday, evening]
Equipment:          [Camera / lens / audio / stabilizer / lighting]
Expected Output:    [Reels / YouTube B-roll / Vlog footage / Photos]
Asset Tags:         [How this will be filed in the library: Travel / Fitness / Trading / etc.]
```

**Shot list format:**

```
Shot 1:  [Description] — [Wide/Medium/Close] — [Duration: X seconds]
Shot 2:  [Description] — [Wide/Medium/Close] — [Duration: X seconds]
[...]
```

Minimum 10 shots planned. Capture 20+. Edit chooses the best.

## Content Library Strategy

**Rule:** Every location visit and trip must produce a minimum of 90 days of content.

This requires: capturing more than is needed, organizing immediately, tagging everything.

### Library Structure

```
/Library
  /Trading
    /Charts-and-Analysis
    /Journal-and-Planning
    /Execution-Moments
  /Business
    /Strategy-Sessions
    /Call-Setups
    /Building-Moments
  /Fitness
    /Training-Sessions
    /Recovery
    /Nutrition
  /Travel
    /[City-Name]
      /Airport
      /Hotel
      /City
  /Lifestyle
    /Morning-Routines
    /Evening-Routines
    /Reading-and-Reflection
  /Brand
    /Talking-Head
    /Testimonials
    /Events
```

**Tagging protocol:** Every file tagged with: date / location / category / subcategory / usability rating (A/B/C)

A = Ready to use as-is
B = Usable with editing
C = Archive only — not for primary use

**Archive everything.** Context that doesn't fit today may be exactly what's needed in six months.
