# Research Frameworks — Reports, Competitors & Trend Analysis

## Research Standard

Every research project answers five questions before conclusions are drawn:

1. What happened? (Factual, evidence-based — no opinion yet)
2. Why did it happen? (Root cause — not surface explanation)
3. Why does it matter to 4x Heist? (Relevance filter)
4. What are the implications? (First and second-order effects)
5. What actions should be taken? (Specific, prioritized)

## Competitor Analysis Framework

Run this quarterly on all primary competitors, and immediately when a competitor makes a significant move.

```
COMPETITOR ANALYSIS — [Name] — [Date]

OVERVIEW:
  Positioning:      [How they present themselves]
  Primary audience: [Who they serve]
  Revenue model:    [How they make money]

OFFER ANALYSIS:
  Core products:    [List with pricing]
  Recent launches:  [New offers in last 90 days]
  Pricing changes:  [Any shifts up or down]
  Free content:     [Lead magnets, free value — what's working]

CONTENT ANALYSIS:
  Top performing:   [Their best recent content — topic, format, engagement]
  Cadence:          [How often they post and where]
  Hook strategies:  [What angles they use]
  Gaps:             [What they don't cover that we could own]

COMMUNITY:
  Size:             [Approximate — Discord, Telegram, YouTube subscribers]
  Engagement:       [Active vs. passive — what's the quality?]
  Culture:          [What does their community feel like?]

STRENGTHS:          [Be honest — what are they doing well?]
WEAKNESSES:         [Where are they vulnerable?]
OPPORTUNITIES:      [What can we do that they can't or don't?]
THREATS:            [What could they do that would hurt us?]

STRATEGIC RESPONSE:
  [What, if anything, should change in our strategy based on this analysis?]
```

## Opportunity Detection Framework

Run this as a standing practice — not just when an obvious opportunity appears.

**The five opportunity questions (ask weekly):**

1. **What is emerging?** What is gaining momentum in the industry that isn't mainstream yet?
2. **What is changing?** What platform, regulation, or market behavior shifted this month?
3. **What is being ignored?** What does everyone in the space overlook that has real value?
4. **What is misunderstood?** What concept is poorly taught or poorly positioned — where we could own the correct framing?
5. **What is becoming valuable?** What skill, product, or audience segment is appreciating in value?

## Trend Evaluation Framework

Before acting on any trend:

```
TREND EVALUATION — [Trend Name]

Description:        [What is this trend?]
Evidence:           [Data, examples, sources — not just one observation]
Origin:             [Where did this start? Is it organic or manufactured?]

Filter:
  Durable?          [Is this structural or cyclical?]
  Useful?           [Does it create genuine value — or just noise?]
  Scalable?         [Can we build something on this that scales?]
  Aligned?          [Does this fit the Sovereign Operator positioning?]
  3-year test:      [Will this still matter in 2029?]
  10-year test:     [Is this a fundamental shift or a fashion?]

Recommendation:     Act / Monitor / Ignore
If Act:             [Specific steps — what exactly should be built or done?]
Timeline:           [When does this window close?]
```

## Consumer Intelligence System

The best intelligence isn't in competitor analysis — it's in the audience's own language.

**Collection sources:**
- Discord and Telegram messages (what questions are being asked repeatedly?)
- YouTube comments (what resonates, what confuses, what do they want more of?)
- DMs and inbound inquiries (what specific pain brought them here?)
- Community conversations (what vocabulary do they use for their problems?)
- Cancellation feedback (why do people leave?)

**Intelligence extracted:**
- Pain point language: the exact words used to describe problems (use in copy)
- Desire language: what outcomes they want and how they describe them (use in positioning)
- Objection patterns: what stops them from buying (address in sales materials)
- Trust signals: what makes them feel safe enough to commit (feature prominently)
