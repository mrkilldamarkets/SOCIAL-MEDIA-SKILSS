## Capital Allocation Framework

**Rule:** Every dollar that enters the business has a job before it arrives.

### Allocation Priority Order

```
Priority 1 — Business Operations (non-negotiable overhead)
  → Software, tools, platforms, broker fees, necessary contractors

Priority 2 — Revenue Expansion (highest ROI deployment)
  → Paid ads, product development, team expansion that directly generates revenue
  → Filter: does this generate >3x return in 90 days?

Priority 3 — Asset Acquisition (owned assets that compound)
  → Real estate (Bahamas target), media assets, IP, software
  → Filter: does this generate cash flow OR appreciate predictably?

Priority 4 — Cash Reserves (protection, not opportunity)
  → Operational reserve (3 months expenses minimum)
  → Emergency reserve (personal, fully liquid)
  → Opportunity capital (ready to deploy — not committed)

Priority 5 — Strategic Investments (external capital deployment)
  → Businesses, syndications, equity positions
  → Only from surplus beyond Priorities 1–4

Priority 6 — Lifestyle (earned, not first)
  → Travel, personal upgrades, experiences
  → Never funded by debt or before reserves are met
```

## Cash Reserve Policy

**Non-negotiable minimums:**

| Reserve Type | Target Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Operational Reserve | 3 months of total business expenses | Business continuity — never touch |
| Emergency Reserve | 3 months of personal expenses | Personal continuity — only for genuine emergencies |
| Opportunity Capital | $10,000–$25,000 liquid | Deploy on high-conviction opportunities |
| Expansion Capital | Variable — set per quarter | Funded growth initiatives only |

**Rule:** Never operate below the operational reserve threshold. If reserves are depleted,
revenue expansion becomes the singular focus until rebuilt. No asset acquisition or lifestyle
spending while below reserve minimums.

## Business Acquisition Filter

Before acquiring any business asset, equity stake, or major capital commitment:

1. Will this generate measurable cash flow within 12 months?
2. Will this increase operational leverage (more output per unit of time/effort)?
3. Does this strengthen the existing ecosystem or is it a distraction?
4. Does this increase enterprise value of the holding company?
5. What is the exit or liquidation path if needed?
6. What is the worst-case scenario and can the business survive it?

**Hard rule:** Never acquire something because it's interesting. Only acquire because it
generates cash flow or builds an asset with compounding value.

## Key Financial Ratios to Track Monthly

| Metric | Target | Alert If |
|---|---|---|
| Profit margin | >40% | <25% |
| Recurring revenue % of total | >50% | <30% |
| Reserves coverage (months) | >3 months | <1.5 months |
| Customer LTV / CAC ratio | >3:1 | <2:1 |
| Revenue concentration risk | No single source >40% | Any source >60% |
