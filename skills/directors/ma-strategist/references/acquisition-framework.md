# Acquisition Framework — Build vs. Buy, Due Diligence & Deal Structure

## Build vs. Buy Decision Framework

Before starting any build, ask whether acquisition is more efficient:

```
BUILD VS. BUY ANALYSIS:

Asset Needed:           [Specific capability or market position]

BUILD PATH:
  Time to build:        [Realistic estimate — not optimistic]
  Cost to build:        $[X] in capital + [X] months of founder attention
  Probability of success: [Honest — most builds take 2–3x longer and cost 2–3x more]
  Competitive risk:     [Can a competitor acquire this during the build window?]

BUY PATH:
  Available targets:    [Specific names or categories to search]
  Estimated cost:       $[X] — multiple of revenue or cash flow
  Time to value:        [How quickly does the acquisition generate return?]
  Integration cost:     [What does operating this require?]

LICENSE / PARTNER PATH:
  Available options:    [Who already has this that we could license or partner with?]
  Cost:                 $[X] per [month/year/deal]
  Flexibility:          [Can exit if it doesn't work]

RECOMMENDATION:         Build / Buy / License / Partner / Wait
RATIONALE:              [One paragraph — honest]
```

**Default bias:** When in doubt, license or partner first. Validate the need before acquiring.

## Acquisition Target Evaluation

### Media Acquisitions (YouTube Channels, Newsletters, Podcasts, Audiences)

```
MEDIA ASSET EVALUATION:

Asset:                  [Name and URL]
Type:                   [YouTube / Newsletter / Community / Podcast / Website]
Audience Size:          [Subscribers / followers / readers]
Audience Quality:       [Niche, engagement rate, demographic alignment]
Monthly Traffic/Views:  [Consistent or declining?]
Revenue:                $[X]/month from [sources]
Revenue Multiple:       [Asking price] ÷ [Annual revenue] = [X]x
Content Library:        [Size, quality, evergreen vs. time-sensitive]
Transferability:        [Does the audience follow the content or the person?]
Synergy Score:          [How directly does this audience map to 4x Heist ecosystem?]
Integration Effort:     [What does running this require post-acquisition?]
Downside:               [What is the worst realistic outcome?]
Asking Price:           $[X]
Offer Range:            $[X] – $[X]
```

**Media acquisition thesis:** The most valuable media acquisitions have an audience that trusts the content — not just a personality. Personality-dependent audiences don't transfer on acquisition.

## Due Diligence Checklist

Before committing to any acquisition:

```
FINANCIAL DUE DILIGENCE:
  □ Revenue verified with bank statements or payment processor exports (last 12 months)
  □ Expenses verified and categorized
  □ Profit margin confirmed — not just gross revenue
  □ Revenue trend: growing / flat / declining (and for how long)
  □ Customer/subscriber concentration risk (does >20% of revenue come from one source?)
  □ Accounts receivable or deferred revenue identified

OPERATIONAL DUE DILIGENCE:
  □ Current team and their role in the business
  □ Tools, software, and subscriptions required to operate (and their costs)
  □ Time requirements per week to maintain at current performance
  □ Vendor and supplier relationships and their transferability

RISK DUE DILIGENCE:
  □ Legal: contracts, IP ownership, outstanding disputes, regulatory exposure
  □ Platform risk: is this asset dependent on a single platform (YouTube, Instagram, etc.)?
  □ Founder risk: does revenue depend on the seller's continued involvement?
  □ Customer/audience churn risk: what is retention and why do people leave?

STRATEGIC DUE DILIGENCE:
  □ Ecosystem synergy confirmed: how specifically does this asset strengthen existing brands?
  □ Integration path defined before LOI is signed
  □ 12-month post-acquisition plan written
```

## Deal Structure Philosophy

**Preferred structures (in order of preference):**

1. **Revenue share** — Aligned incentives. Seller participates in growth.
2. **Earn-out** — Portion of purchase price tied to future performance.
3. **Milestone-based payments** — Tranches released as integration targets are hit.
4. **Licensing** — Access without full ownership commitment.
5. **Minority stake** — Participate in upside without full integration complexity.
6. **Full acquisition** — Used when the asset is proven, integration is clear, and price is right.

**Avoid:** Large upfront cash acquisitions of unproven assets / Deals that drain reserves /
Acquisitions that require operational involvement before systems are in place
