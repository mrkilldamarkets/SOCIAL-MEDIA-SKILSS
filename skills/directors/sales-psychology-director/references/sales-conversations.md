# Sales Conversations — Discovery Calls, DMs & Investor Psychology

## Discovery Call Framework (HTBB / Operator Mentorship)

**Duration:** 30–45 minutes
**Objective:** Understand their situation → determine fit → create clarity for a decision

```
OPENING (2 minutes):
  "Before we get into anything about the program — I want to understand where you're at.
  Tell me about your trading right now. What does a typical week look like?"
  [Listen. Take notes. Do not interrupt. Let them fully express.]

SITUATION DEEP DIVE (10 minutes):
  "How long have you been dealing with [main issue they mentioned]?"
  "What have you tried so far to fix that?"
  "What's working? What isn't?"
  [Understand their attempted solutions — this reveals their sophistication level and shows you've listened]

GOALS AND STAKES (8 minutes):
  "What does success look like for you — specifically? In 6 months, what would need to have changed?"
  "What happens if the next 6 months look the same as the last 6?"
  [This creates the pain/desire frame — they articulate the stakes themselves]

ALIGNMENT CHECK (5 minutes):
  "Based on what you've told me, [program] sounds like it could be a strong fit because [specific reason]."
  "Before I walk you through what's included — do you have any questions about whether this is right for you?"
  [Do not pitch yet. Check for objections. Surface them now, not after the presentation.]

PROGRAM OVERVIEW (8 minutes):
  Walk through the program in terms of their situation — not a feature list.
  "The reason [X component] exists is specifically because of what you described — [their problem]."
  Always connect features to their stated goals.

DECISION FACILITATION (5 minutes):
  "Does this seem like what you've been looking for?"
  If yes: "The next step is [specific action]."
  If hesitation: "What's your biggest concern right now?"
  [Surface the objection and address it — then return]

CLOSE / NEXT STEP (2 minutes):
  One clear action. Application link / payment link / follow-up date.
  Never leave without a defined next step — even if it's "I'll follow up Thursday."
```

**Call rules:**
- Talk less than the prospect in the first half — their words matter more than yours
- Never pitch before you understand the situation
- Never push after two clear hesitation signals — disqualify gracefully and offer to reconnect later

## DM Sales Framework

**Purpose:** Qualify, understand, guide — never hard close in DMs.

**Response to trigger word (OPERATOR / HEIST / HTBB):**

```
First message:
"Thanks for reaching out — happy to share more details.
Quick question before I do: what made you reach out today? What's the main thing you're looking to change?"

[Wait for their response — do not send the program information until you understand their situation]

Second message (after they respond):
"Got it. So [restate their situation in their language] — that's exactly what [program] is designed for.
Let me send you the details. But first — [one qualifying question: timeline / experience / commitment]."

[After they respond to qualification]:
"Here's how it works: [brief, specific overview in 2–3 sentences]
The next step for people who are serious is [application / join link / discovery call].
Does that sound like something you want to explore?"
```

**DM pacing rules:**
- One question per message — multiple questions get ignored
- Never send a wall of text — short messages that invite responses
- If they go quiet: one follow-up after 48 hours ("Hey [name] — did you get a chance to look this over?")
- If no response after follow-up: no third message. Release.

## Investor Psychology (Blue Marlin)

Investors have a fundamentally different psychology than coaching or education buyers.

**What investors prioritize (in order):**

1. **Trust in the manager** — Is this person who they say they are? Are they consistent?
2. **Risk management** — Not "how much will I make" but "how much could I lose and how is it handled?"
3. **Transparency** — Will they know what's happening at all times?
4. **Professionalism** — Does this feel like a proper institutional operation?
5. **Consistency** — Not necessarily returns — consistency of communication, reporting, process

**What investors are really afraid of:**
- Fraud or misrepresentation (higher fear than underperformance)
- Lack of communication during drawdown (silence terrifies investors)
- Not understanding what's happening to their capital
- Making a mistake that reflects poorly on their financial judgment

**Discovery call psychology for investors:**

In the first half: listen. Understand their investment experience, goals, and concerns.
Never pitch the strategy before understanding their risk tolerance.

The investor who says they're comfortable with risk often isn't. Test it: "If you saw your account down 15% in a month, what would your first instinct be?" The answer is more reliable than self-reporting.

**The closing moment for investors:**
Investors don't close like service buyers. They move slowly and deliberately.
After the call, the IIM, and any follow-up questions — there is a moment where they say "I want to move forward."
That moment is created by trust accumulated over the entire relationship — not a single conversation.

Patience is a sales skill in institutional contexts. The investor who takes two months to commit is often the most loyal, highest-AUM, best-referring investor in the portfolio.

## High-Ticket Conversation Design (Operator Mentorship — $5,000/month)

At $5,000/month, buyers are not impulsive. They are making a considered identity decision.

**The three things that must be true before they commit:**

1. **Certainty that you can produce the result** — Not confidence in general. Confidence in their specific situation.
   "Based on what you've told me about [their specific situation], here's why this works for someone at your stage: [specific reason]."

2. **Clarity that this is different from what they've already tried** — High-ticket buyers have almost always tried lower-cost alternatives.
   You must address this directly: "What have you done in the past that didn't produce the result you wanted?"
   Then connect your mechanism to why those previous solutions didn't work.

3. **Alignment of investment with the stakes** — $5,000/month feels different when framed against what it's solving.
   "You mentioned you've been losing [X] months of the year to inconsistency. What does a consistent funded income mean to your life?"
   Let them articulate the value — then the price is contextualized by their own words, not yours.

**Non-negotiable at this price point:** Zero discount policy. Full price or not at all.
A prospect who asks for a discount and is accommodated has less confidence in the product, not more.
The correct response: "The program is [price]. That's the investment. What I can offer is [additional value if appropriate] — but the price doesn't change."
