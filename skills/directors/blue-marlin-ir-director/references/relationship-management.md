# Relationship Management — Retention, Allocations & Events

## Investor Retention Philosophy

Retention is built in the quiet months, not the winning months.

When returns are strong, investors stay automatically. The relationship is tested during:
- Drawdown periods
- Extended flat performance
- Market volatility and uncertainty
- Silence (the absence of communication destroys relationships faster than bad performance)

Retention strategy is therefore: proactive communication architecture, not reactive damage control.

---

## Investor Communication Calendar

Minimum touchpoint schedule per active investor:

| Frequency | Type | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Performance report | First week of each month |
| Quarterly | Quarterly review | First two weeks of new quarter |
| Bi-annual | Relationship call | June and December |
| Event-driven | Drawdown communication | Any drawdown exceeding 10% |
| Event-driven | Market update | Major macro event affecting strategy |
| Annual | Year-end letter | December — personal, reflective, forward-looking |

**No-surprise rule:** Investors should never hear about a significant drawdown in the monthly
report for the first time. If a drawdown is developing mid-month, communicate proactively.
"I wanted to reach out before the end of month report because I want to ensure you have
context on what's happening." This one practice retains more investors than any other.

## CRM Relationship Tracking

Beyond pipeline data, track relationship intelligence:

```
Investor Goals (current — reviewed quarterly)
Risk Tolerance (documented + date last confirmed)
Capital Allocated per Strategy
Broker Account Status
Communication Preference (email / phone / WhatsApp)
Key Personal Context (business, family context where shared — human notes)
Questions History (every significant question they've ever asked)
Concerns History (every concern raised + how it was resolved)
Future Allocation Potential (estimated additional capital available)
Referral Potential (likelihood + who they might introduce)
Retention Risk Level (Low / Medium / High — reviewed monthly)
Last Personal Touchpoint (not report delivery — actual conversation)
```

**Retention risk flags:** Set to High when:
- No response to last two communications
- Expressed dissatisfaction or concern without resolution
- Account in drawdown >15%
- Personal situation change that may affect capital availability
- Six or more months since last direct conversation

## Additional Allocation Strategy

**Rule:** Never ask for more capital. Build the conditions where the investor offers it.

The conditions that naturally produce additional allocations:

1. **Consistent reporting** — investor sees reliable professionalism every month
2. **Transparent drawdown communication** — investor sees the risk management working in real time
3. **Compounding visible in account** — investor watches equity grow and wants more in the system
4. **Relationship depth** — investor feels like a partner, not a transaction

**Planting the seed (without pressure):**

In quarterly reviews, include a section on capacity:
"The strategy currently has capacity for additional allocations. If your financial situation
has changed or you have capital you're considering deploying, I'm happy to discuss how additional
allocation would work within your existing account structure."

This is informational. It is not a pitch. One sentence. Once per quarter maximum.

## Drawdown Management Protocol

This is the highest-stakes investor relations scenario. Execution here determines whether an
investor becomes a long-term partner or a one-allocation relationship.

### Drawdown Communication Triggers

| Drawdown Level | Action |
|---|---|
| 5–10% | Monthly report context sufficient. No separate communication required. |
| 10–15% | Proactive email before month-end report. Context + risk management confirmation. |
| 15–20% | Email + call offered. Detailed explanation of conditions and response. |
| 20%+ | Call required (not offered — initiated). Full transparency. No spin. |

### Drawdown Communication Framework

**Structure:**
1. Acknowledge directly — no burying it in section three of a report
2. Explain the market conditions that caused it (factual, not excuse-making)
3. Describe exactly what risk management actions were taken
4. State current exposure and drawdown status
5. Explain the recovery path and what conditions need to change
6. Invite questions — make it easy to respond

**Tone:** Calm. Factual. Direct. Not apologetic to the point of sounding panicked.
"This is the risk. This is how we're managing it. This is what we expect going forward."

**What not to do during drawdown:**
- Go quiet (the worst possible response)
- Over-promise recovery timelines
- Blame external factors without owning the execution
- Add fake positivity to soften the communication

## Investor Events

### Quarterly Investor Call (Virtual)
- Format: 45–60 minutes. JorDache and Sultan presenting.
- Structure: Performance review (20 min) / Market outlook (15 min) / Q&A (20 min)
- Recording available to investors who cannot attend live
- Frequency: Quarterly — aligned with quarterly report cycle

### Research Briefings
- Format: 30–45 minute deep-dive on a specific market theme
- Audience: All active investors
- Frequency: When a significant market development warrants it (not scheduled — event-driven)
- Purpose: Positions Blue Marlin as a research-driven institution, not just a trading operation

### Private Webinars
- Invitation-only
- For investors considering additional allocations or referral candidates
- Content: More detailed strategy overview, live Q&A
- Frequency: Quarterly

### Annual Investor Gathering
- In-person (when geography allows — Bahamas preferred, or wherever JorDache is based)
- Small group (10–20 investors maximum)
- Format: Dinner or private event
- Purpose: Relationship deepening, not performance review
- This is the highest-leverage retention investment in the entire IR calendar

## Year-End Letter

Delivered in December. Personal. Reflective. Not a report.

Structure:
- Personal note from JorDache on the year — what was learned, what was built
- One paragraph on the year's market environment
- One paragraph on the firm's growth and development
- Forward vision for the coming year
- Genuine expression of appreciation for the investor relationship

Tone: Founder's letter. Not corporate. Not marketing. Warm and institutional simultaneously.

This letter does more for retention than any report. Write it with care.
