# Legacy Systems — Succession, Governance & Global Mobility

## Succession Planning Philosophy

Build systems that survive: market cycles / economic crises / founder absence /
leadership changes / generational transitions.

A family office that requires the founder's daily involvement is a job, not a legacy.
The objective is a system that operates, grows, and compounds regardless of any individual.

---

## Succession Framework

### Operating Company Succession
Each operating company must have:
- A documented operating manual (systems that don't live in one person's head)
- At least one person who can step into the primary operating role
- Clear decision-making hierarchy that doesn't require founder escalation for routine decisions

**Succession timeline:** Begin building this infrastructure at $1M ARR per company.
Every year delayed is a year the business remains founder-dependent.

### Family Office Succession
- Family office governance structure documented (who manages what, how decisions are made)
- Trusted advisors identified: attorney / accountant / financial planner / estate planner
- Legal structures in place: trusts, entities, beneficiary designations
- Estate plan reviewed annually — not set and forgotten

---

## Family Governance Framework

The family office governs itself by a clear philosophy:

**Encourage:** Responsibility / Contribution / Performance / Discipline / Ownership / Growth

**Never tolerate:** Entitlement / Dependency / Victim mentality / Passivity

### The Darling Family Standard

Every family member who benefits from family office assets should understand:
1. Where the wealth came from and what it required
2. Their responsibility to steward, not consume it
3. The expectation of contribution — not entitlement
4. That access to capital increases with demonstrated responsibility

**Education before inheritance:** Future generations should inherit knowledge and standards
before they inherit capital. Capital without capability destroys both.

## Family Education System

What future generations should be taught — not assumed to know:

```
Financial literacy:
  - How money is made (income, business, assets — the hierarchy)
  - How businesses operate at a fundamental level
  - How to read a financial statement
  - How compounding works and why it's non-linear

Character development:
  - Discipline as identity (the Sovereign Operator standard applied to family life)
  - Accountability without external enforcement
  - Long-term thinking over immediate gratification
  - Contribution as the source of self-respect

Business education:
  - Real exposure to how the businesses operate (age-appropriate)
  - Involvement in family capital decisions at appropriate levels
  - Entrepreneurial thinking — how to create value, not just receive it

Legacy understanding:
  - Family history and how the current position was built
  - The responsibility that comes with family office resources
  - The expectation to maintain and grow — not just consume
```
