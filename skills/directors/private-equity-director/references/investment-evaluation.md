# Investment Evaluation — Business Quality, Cash Flow & Deal Structure

## Business Quality Framework

The six questions that determine whether a business is investment-grade:

```
1. Does this generate recurring revenue?
   (Subscription, retainer, performance fee — not one-time transactional revenue)

2. Does it have pricing power?
   (Can it raise prices without losing customers? If not — it's a commodity.)

3. Does it have customer loyalty?
   (Would customers notice if this disappeared? High switching cost = durable.)

4. Can it operate without the founder?
   (Founder-dependent = not an asset, it's a job with equity attached)

5. Does it have competitive advantages?
   (Brand, community, technology, data, network effects — what stops a competitor from copying?)

6. Can it scale?
   (Does revenue grow faster than costs? Or does every dollar of revenue require a dollar of labor?)
```

**Scoring:** 5–6 yes answers = high quality. 3–4 = acceptable with clear improvement path.
Fewer than 3 = do not invest regardless of how compelling the narrative sounds.

## Competitive Advantage Evaluation

Look for one or more of these moats:

| Moat Type | What It Means | How to Verify |
|---|---|---|
| Brand | Customers pay a premium for the name alone | Pricing power without losing customers |
| Distribution | Access to a customer channel competitors can't easily replicate | Proprietary audience, community, or channel |
| Community | Network where value increases as membership grows | Churn rate, engagement metrics, member-to-member value |
| Technology | Proprietary software or systems difficult to rebuild | Time and cost to replicate; patent or trade secret protection |
| Data | Accumulated data that improves the product or creates insight competitors can't access | Proprietary dataset, track record, research |
| Network Effects | The product gets better as more people use it | Does one user's presence make it more valuable for others? |
| Operational Excellence | Consistently executes better than competitors at lower cost | Margins compared to industry, customer satisfaction |

**The moat test:** If a well-funded competitor entered this market tomorrow, how long would it take them to meaningfully hurt this business? Less than 12 months = weak moat. 3–5 years = strong moat. 10+ years = exceptional.

## Deal Structure Options

Evaluate in this order — simpler structures preferred:

```
1. Revenue Share
   Seller receives X% of revenue for Y months post-acquisition.
   Best for: Businesses where seller's involvement aids transition.
   Risk: Seller motivation misaligned if not structured carefully.

2. Earn-Out
   Purchase price includes a base payment + additional amount tied to hitting performance milestones.
   Best for: When future performance is uncertain but upside is real.
   Risk: Disputes over metrics — define them precisely.

3. Milestone-Based Payments
   Purchase price released in tranches as integration targets are achieved.
   Best for: Protecting capital while ensuring transition quality.

4. Minority Equity Purchase
   Buy X% ownership without controlling interest.
   Best for: Early-stage relationships, strategic alignment without full integration complexity.

5. Majority Equity Purchase
   Buy controlling stake. Full operational authority.
   Best for: Businesses ready for integration and improvement plan execution.

6. Full Cash Acquisition
   Clean purchase — seller exits, buyer controls.
   Best for: Simple businesses with clear systems and no transition risk.
```

**Default:** Start with the simplest structure that achieves the goal. Complexity creates friction.

## Value Creation Playbook (Post-Acquisition)

After acquiring any asset, run this 90-day value creation sprint:

```
DAYS 1–30: UNDERSTAND
  □ Document all existing processes (as-is, not how we wish they worked)
  □ Identify the three most critical revenue drivers
  □ Identify the three biggest operational weaknesses
  □ Map all costs — eliminate anything with no clear revenue connection
  □ Meet key customers/members — listen before changing anything

DAYS 31–60: STABILIZE
  □ Fix the highest-urgency weakness
  □ Establish reporting metrics (revenue, churn, engagement — weekly minimum)
  □ Onboard team into ecosystem tools and standards
  □ Identify one quick-win revenue opportunity and execute it

DAYS 61–90: OPTIMIZE
  □ Implement first major operational improvement
  □ Integrate with existing ecosystem (cross-promotion, shared infrastructure, CRM)
  □ Set 12-month performance targets
  □ Define value creation roadmap with milestones
```

**Key principle:** Don't change everything at once. The business was generating value before the acquisition.
Understand why before disrupting.
