# Blue Marlin Securities — Content Strategy & Language System

## Content Philosophy

Blue Marlin Securities does not create content. It publishes intelligence.

There is a fundamental difference:
- Content is created to fill a feed.
- Intelligence is published because it matters.

Every output from Blue Marlin should feel like it was written for people who already operate at a high level
and simply need confirmation that this institution understands the world the same way they do.

---

## What Blue Marlin Publishes

**Research** — Market analysis, macro positioning, sector intelligence
**Frameworks** — Mental models for capital allocation, risk, and decision-making
**Insights** — Observations about markets, institutions, capital flows, and global dynamics
**Thought Leadership** — Long-form perspectives on finance, sovereignty, and wealth architecture

**Never:**
- Beginner education
- Engagement bait
- Trend chasing
- Controversy
- Tutorial content
- Retail-targeted trading tips

## Language System

### Use

- Precision over personality
- Authority without arrogance
- Clarity without simplification
- Confidence without aggression
- Brevity as sophistication

### Sentence Construction

Short. Declarative. Intentional.

"The market is not wrong. It is pricing uncertainty differently than you are."

Not:

"In today's uncertain macroeconomic environment, investors are increasingly looking at various factors
that may or may not impact their portfolio in ways that could be significant depending on..."

---

### Forbidden Language

| Avoid | Because |
|---|---|
| "Revolutionary" | Hype |
| "Excited to share" | Amateur |
| "Alpha" (as slang) | Retail finance vernacular |
| "DYOR" / "NFA" | Retail disclaimer culture |
| "To the moon" | Obvious |
| "Game-changing" | Startup energy |
| Exclamation marks | Never in institutional writing |
| Emoji in formal content | Never |
| "We believe" | Show it, don't declare it |

## Research Publication Format

**Structure:**

```
Headline     — Declarative. One sentence. No question marks.
Subheading   — Context in one line.
Body         — 3–5 paragraphs maximum for digital. Longer for formal research notes.
Framework    — If applicable: visual model, table, or structured observation
Close        — Implication or forward-looking statement. Never a call to action.
```

**Design:**
- Dark background
- Financial Times-style editorial layout
- Gold category labels (e.g., "MACRO" / "FIXED INCOME" / "CAPITAL MARKETS")
- Author byline: name and title only. No photo in digital research cards.

---

## Email Communications

For private client or investor communications:

- No marketing templates
- Plain institutional formatting — dark or white background depending on context
- Never opens with "Hey" or "Hi [First Name]!"
- Opens with the intelligence itself — no preamble
- Closes without a CTA unless absolutely necessary
- Signature: Name / Title / Blue Marlin Securities / Contact

---

## Content Evaluation Criteria

Every piece of Blue Marlin content is evaluated against:

1. Does this increase institutional trust?
2. Does this reinforce the Sovereign Navigator positioning?
3. Would a sophisticated investor find this credible?
4. Does this feel different from retail finance content?
5. Does it reflect the precision of the Blue Marlin philosophy?

If any answer is no — revise before publishing.
