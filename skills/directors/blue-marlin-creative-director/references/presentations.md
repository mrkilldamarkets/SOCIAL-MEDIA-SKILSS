# Blue Marlin Securities — Presentations & Investor Materials

## Core Standard

Every investor presentation must feel like a $10 Billion family office deck.
Not a startup pitch. Not a broker deck. Not a prop firm overview.

A sophisticated institution presenting quietly to people who already understand the game.

---

## Slide Philosophy

Every slide must feel: Calm / Confident / Sophisticated / Institutional / Strategic

**One idea per slide.** Brevity is sophistication.
**Data speaks.** Annotations are minimal.
**Silence earns trust faster than explanation.**

---

## Visual Standards for Slides

- Background: Deep Ocean Blue (#071A35) or Midnight Navy (#0B1220)
- Text: Pure White (#FFFFFF) for headlines, rgba(255,255,255,0.75) for body
- Accents: Gold (#D4AF37) for key metrics, dividers, section markers
- Layout: Generous whitespace. Never crowded. Never template.
- Charts: Minimal. Dark backgrounds. Single color lines. Gold highlight on key data.
- No gradients that look cheap. No drop shadows that look amateur.

---

## Investor Deck Structure

### Cover
- Blue Marlin mark (centered or top-left)
- Presentation title (large, understated)
- Date and classification (e.g., "Confidential — For Qualified Investors")
- No taglines on the cover. The logo is the statement.

### Section 01 — The Philosophy
Brief. 2–3 slides maximum.
Introduce "The Ocean Rewards Precision." Frame the firm through the Marlin philosophy.
This is not a mission statement. It is a worldview.

### Section 02 — The Firm
What Blue Marlin Securities is. What it does. What it is not.
Positioning: Modern family office / Institutional research / Capital intelligence.
No buzzwords. No hype. Clean, confident prose.

### Section 03 — Capital Strategy
Performance data, strategy overview, risk framework.
All data presented with institutional formatting — not retail charting aesthetics.
Tables over infographics. Clarity over visualization for its own sake.

### Section 04 — Research Infrastructure
How intelligence is generated. Market coverage. Research depth.
If applicable: technology stack, data sources, analytical frameworks.

### Section 05 — The Team
Minimal. Photographs if used must be institutional quality — not LinkedIn headshots.
Titles without ego. Credentials without decoration.

### Section 06 — The Opportunity
What you are offering. To whom. On what terms.
This is the ask — but it should not feel like an ask.
It should feel like an invitation to something private.

### Section 07 — Close
Single slide. Contact information. Nothing else.
"Private inquiries: [email]"

## Language System for Investor Materials

**Use:** Precision / Authority / Clarity / Confidence / Simplicity

**Avoid:** Hype / Urgency / Aggressive sales / Internet slang / Retail finance language

**Tone references:** Financial Times editorial / Private placement memorandums / Family office letters

**Sentence structure:** Short. Declarative. Never breathless.

**Forbidden phrases in investor materials:**
- "Revolutionary" / "Game-changing" / "Disrupting"
- "Excited to announce"
- "We believe" (show, don't declare)
- Any phrase a startup would use

---

## Data Presentation Standards

- All performance figures presented net of fees
- Benchmark comparisons included where relevant
- Risk metrics always accompany return figures
- Time periods clearly labeled
- No cherry-picked windows without context

**Chart style:**
- Dark background
- Single metric per chart unless comparison is the point
- Gold line for primary metric
- Subtle grid lines (rgba white, low opacity)
- Clean axis labels — no chart junk
